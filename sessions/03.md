  **Questions:**
      Question 1: Why are we interested in being able to identify the ori within a genome from its nucleotide sequence alone?
        Suggested video: [Origin of Replication Sites by LabXchange](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PHLGTA6lrro)
          - The video is a little dense -- the beginning through 3:22 is the part to focus on

      Question 2: Can you recover the nucleotide sequence of one DNA strand losslessly if you know the sequence of its complementary strand? In other words, if you retain one strand, have you lost any information about the identities of the paired nucleotides?
        Suggested video: [Compression, Lossy & Lossless by Professor Cunningham](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mNRdjGm03pI)
          - He reviews lossy and lossless compression by focusing on a more complex version of this problem: encoding a Taylor Swift song

      Question 3: How could you translate Legrand's reasoning about the Gold Bug into an algorithm that could solve this kind of cipher efficiently?
        Suggested video: [Cryptography. Edgar Allan Poe, the Gold Bug, and Cryptanalysis by Andrew Octopus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XmWudrY0hlM)
        - He goes into more detail about the Gold Bug story in a cryptographic context. Looking ahead, it might be interesting to think about the frequency table he shows -- how does it differ from how the textbook describes Legrand's solution?
